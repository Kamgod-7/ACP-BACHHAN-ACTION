Gangsta Bachlun

The street ahead is never really empty for long. Someone's always stepping out from a doorway or rounding the corner, and in this city, almost none of them are there to talk. Gangsta Bachlun drops you straight into that pressure and doesn't let up.

Quick Overview

  • A one-tap shooting game about clearing a crime-heavy city, one thug at a time
  • Enemies approach with swords, pistols, or bombs strapped to their bodies
  • Endless mode — no levels to finish, just a kill count that keeps climbing until someone gets too close

The Story: One Man Against a City Full of Thugs

Bachlun isn't a cop, and he isn't waiting for one to show up either. He's a gangster with his own idea of cleaning house, and the city he's working through is packed with criminals who aren't going anywhere on their own. Every street he walks down has more of them waiting, which is really the whole premise of the game — there's no shortage of trouble here, and Bachlun's job is to make sure none of it reaches him first.

There's no real finish line to this story. The city keeps producing more thugs than one man can realistically deal with, so the fight just continues for as long as you can keep up with it.

How the Shooting Actually Works

Enemies walk in from the front of the screen, and no two of them arrive the same way. Some come in swinging a sword, closing the distance fast and relying on getting close enough to strike. Others hang back a little and start firing pistols the moment they've got a clear shot. Bachlun handles all of it with a shotgun, which is powerful enough to drop most threats in a single well-placed hit.

The actual objective is simple to describe and harder to execute once things speed up: don't let anyone reach you. Every enemy that gets close is a problem, but some are a much bigger problem than others.

The Bomb Threat Changes Everything

Mixed into the usual sword-and-pistol crowd are enemies rigged with bombs strapped to their bodies. They don't shoot, and they're not trying to slash at you with a blade — they're walking straight at you on purpose, and if they make it into range, they detonate. That explosion takes Bachlun down with them, and the run ends right there.

This is really the mechanic that shapes how you play. A pistol enemy left alive for an extra second might get a shot off. A bomber left alive for an extra second could end the entire game. Spotting one early and dropping it from a distance matters more than clearing every other enemy on screen first.

Controls

The whole game runs on one input, which keeps things fast without making them mindless.

Action Control
Shoot Enemy Tap the enemy on screen

That's the entire control scheme — no separate aim stick, no charging a shot, no menu to dig through mid-fight. Bachlun fires the instant you tap a target, and a clean hit takes that enemy out immediately.

It sounds almost too simple until three enemies show up from different angles at once, and suddenly deciding who to tap first becomes the actual skill being tested.

Tips That Actually Help

  • Scan for bomb carriers first, every single wave. A sword enemy two steps closer is annoying. A bomber two steps closer is a lost run.
  • Don't fixate on whichever enemy is easiest to tap. The one closest to detonation range deserves the shot, even if it means letting a slower threat wait a beat longer.
  • Pistol enemies are dangerous at range, but they're rarely the reason a run ends. Treat them as a lower priority than anything walking toward you with a bomb strapped on.
  • Keep your taps quick and deliberate rather than rapid and careless. A missed tap on a bomber is worse than a slightly slow one that actually connects.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • The one-tap control scheme makes it easy to pick up instantly, even for players who've never touched a shooter before
  • Mixing sword, pistol, and bomb enemies keeps every wave from feeling like a repeat of the last one
  • The kill-count scoring gives every run a clear number to try and beat

Cons

  • A single missed bomber can end a strong run almost instantly, which takes some getting used to
  • There's no level structure for players who'd rather chase a fixed goal than an open-ended score
